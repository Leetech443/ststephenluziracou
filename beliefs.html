The Holy Bible

2 Timothy 3:16–17 | 2 Peter 1:20–21 | Psalm 119:160

The Bible is God’s inspired Word. Its truths did not begin with people but with God, who spoke through the human authors by the Holy Spirit. We receive the sixty-six books of the Old and New Testaments as containing all things necessary for salvation and as the final authority for our faith and conduct.

The Holy Trinity

Matthew 28:19 | 2 Corinthians 13:14 | 1 Peter 1:2

We believe in one God, eternally existing in three persons: Father, Son and Holy Spirit. Equal in power and glory, the Trinity is confessed in the Apostles’ Creed and the Nicene Creed, which we say together in worship.

Jesus Christ

John 1:1–14 | Hebrews 4:14–15 | Acts 2:32–33

Jesus of Nazareth is the eternal Son of God who became man to save sinners. Born of the Virgin Mary and without sin, he proclaimed the kingdom of God, died on the cross for our sins, rose again on the third day and ascended to the Father, where he is our great High Priest.

The Holy Spirit

John 14:26 | Acts 1:8 | Galatians 5:22–23

The Holy Spirit brings new life, convicts us of sin and draws us to Christ. He empowers believers for witness and service, produces the fruit of Christian character, and guides the Church into all truth.

Salvation by Grace

Ephesians 2:8–9 | Romans 3:23 | Romans 10:9–13

All have sinned and fall short of God’s glory, and sin separates us from him. Jesus took our sins upon himself, died in our place and rose again so that we might be forgiven and receive eternal life. We receive this gift by grace through faith: we repent, believe in Christ and confess him as Lord.

Baptism

Matthew 28:19–20 | Acts 2:38–41 | Romans 6:3–4

Baptism is the sacrament of new birth, an outward sign of an inward change. By it we are joined to Christ in his death and resurrection and welcomed into his Church. All who are baptised are called to live out their faith each day.

Holy Communion

Luke 22:14–19 | Acts 2:42 | 1 Corinthians 11:23–26

At the Lord’s Table we share bread and wine in remembrance of Christ’s death for us. It is a sacrament of his love, in which we receive him by faith, are renewed in fellowship with him and with one another, and look forward to his return.

The Church

Ephesians 4:1–6 | Acts 2:42–47 | Matthew 16:18

The Church is the body of Christ, called out of every nation and tribe. As part of the Anglican Communion, we gather to worship, to be taught from God’s Word, to share fellowship and the sacraments, and to carry the good news into the world.

His Return & Eternal Life

1 Thessalonians 4:13–18 | Revelation 21:1–5 | John 14:1–3

Jesus will return in glory to judge the living and the dead. Those who belong to him will be raised to eternal life in the new heaven and the new earth, where God will wipe away every tear and make all things new.
